PyroSphere

AI-enabled geospatial monitoring of industrial thermal anomalies

What is PyroSphere

Turning a global noise floor of fires into an industrial signal.

PyroSphere ingests near-real-time thermal anomalies from NASA FIRMS (VIIRS), then enriches each detection with geospatial context from ~15,800 known industrial facilities — power plants, refineries, steel and cement works — and its own on-location temporal history.

What comes out is not another fire feed. It is a curated, defensible list of industrial-classified thermal hotspots, each with human-readable reasons, ready for an analyst to follow up with one click.

The Problem

A single VIIRS detection is ambiguous.

One thermal anomaly can be a gas flare at an LNG terminal, a persistent process heat source at a steel mill, an industrial accident — or simply a wildfire, agricultural residue burn, or routine crop clearing.

Faced with hundreds of thousands of raw detections, an analyst has no way to separate signal from noise. Existing FIRMS viewers show every fire and offer no industrial lens. PyroSphere narrows the world down to what matters.

How It Works

A transparent pipeline, from satellite to signal.

  • 01

    NASA FIRMS thermal anomalies

    Global VIIRS NRT detections are streamed in and pre-screened for industrial context before storage.

  • 02

    Industrial facility context

    OpenStreetMap + WRI power-plant geometry: 15,809 indexed facilities provide the geospatial anchor.

  • 03

    Geospatial enrichment

    Each hotspot is matched to the nearest facility, inside/outside geometry, bounding distance and drivers.

  • 04

    Classification

    Transparent rules fuse temporal persistence, FRP baselines and proximity into gas flare, persistent source or industrial fire — with reasons.

  • 05

    Interactive GIS monitoring

    Deep-dive on a globe: filter by class, inspect per-hotspot evidence and follow facilities.

Data Sources

Open, explainable, auditable.

  • NASA FIRMS / VIIRS — near-real-time thermal anomaly detections
  • OpenStreetMap — industrial and power infrastructure geometry
  • WRI Global Power Plant Database — global power facility registry
  • VIIRS Nightfire / thermal context — persistent combustion signatures
  • Land-cover & contextual satellite data — future enrichment seams
